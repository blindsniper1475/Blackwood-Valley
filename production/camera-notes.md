# CAMERA NOTES — BLACKWOOD VALLEY

## Carol's Camera: The Mamiya RB67
- Medium-format film camera. Heavy. Mechanical. Manual everything.
- 12 shots per roll (6x7 format).
- Every shot is deliberate. She doesn't spray. She composes.
- The advance lever CLACK after each shot is a rhythmic motif.
- She counts her shots. The audience should feel the tension of running low.

## The Flash: External Strobe
- Heavy, bulky, mounted on top of the camera.
- Blinding. Physical. Leaves a ringing in the ears.
- Illuminates the entire room for a split second, then darkness rushes back.
- In the shelter (Ep 1), it fires blue-purple instead of white. Anomaly.
- This is the ONLY light burst in the series. No continuous flash.

## The Lantern: LED Camping Lantern
- Steady, bright, white. No flame. No flicker.
- This is Carol's primary light source for exploration.
- It does NOT flicker. It does NOT go out. It is reliable.
- The contrast: warm LED glow vs. cold blue moonlight/rain outside.

## Cinematography (The Viewer's Camera)
- 35mm lens feel. Shallow depth of field.
- Film grain. Naturalistic. Not polished. Not clean.
- Handheld when Carol moves. Locked when she's still.
- The lantern is the primary light source in frame.
- The strobe flash is the ONLY moment of full illumination.

## Lighting Palette
- Outside: Cold blue moonlight + rain. Grey. Wet.
- Inside (house): Warm LED lantern + cold TV static + amber clock glow.
- Inside (shelter): LED lantern only. Green walls. Dry. Still.
- RV: Red safelight (darkroom). Warm. Intimate. Womb-like.

## The "One Light" Motif
- The house has ONE bathroom light on. Just one.
- The porch light is dead. The kitchen is dark. The bedrooms are dark.
- Only the bathroom. Only one. This is a visual motif.
- The audience should always be aware of where the light is and isn't.
