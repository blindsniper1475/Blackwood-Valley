# BLACKWOOD VALLEY — SERIES BIBLE

## Logline

Carol Mercer, an urbex blogger, moves into an evacuated town in Washington State
and spends weeks documenting what's left behind — only to discover that some
things in this town refuse to die, and the reason the evacuation order was never
rescinded is worse than anyone remembers.

## Core Themes

- **Analog Preservation vs. Organic Decay:** The town's infrastructure is caught
  in a spotty stasis field. The wiring, the plumbing, the electrical grid — preserved.
  The couch, the carpet, the clothes — rotting. The skeleton survives; the flesh doesn't.
- **Bureaucratic Horror:** The town wasn't sealed by a curse. It was sealed by
  paperwork. The tragedy is neglect, not malice.
- **The Weight of Ordinary Lives:** Every location is a monument to someone's
  Tuesday afternoon. The horror isn't that people died here. It's that they lived.
- **The Documentarian's Burden:** Carol is the only witness. Without her, these
  places simply cease. She carries the responsibility of remembering.

## Genre & Tone

- Grounded psychological mystery. NOT a ghost story.
- Melancholic, atmospheric, patient.
- The horror comes from implication, not revelation.
- Think: True Detective Season 1 meets Every Frame a Painting meets Annihilation.

## The Protagonist — Carol Mercer

- Mid-thirties. Urbex blogger/photographer.
- Talks to her audience like there's a friend standing next to her.
- Curious, respectful, a little funny, quietly unsettled. Never sensational.
- She came prepared: supplies for weeks, a satellite link, a map full of targets.

### Carol's Gear (Analog-First)

| Item | Details |
|------|---------|
| Camera | Medium-format film camera (Mamiya RB67 or similar). Mechanical. Manual advance. 12 shots per roll. |
| Flash | Heavy external strobe. Blinding, physical, leaves a ringing in the ears. |
| Audio | Professional microcassette field recorder. Physical buttons. Tape hiss. |
| Light | Rechargeable LED camping lantern. Steady, bright, white. NO flame. NO flashlight. |
| Clothing | Heavy waxed-canvas hooded cloak. Waterproof. Worn soft at the edges. |
| Navigation | Paper map of Blackwood County, annotated, locations circled in red marker. |

### The RV — Home Base

- 1980s Winnebago. Battered, rusted, faded chevrons. Functional.
- Interior: stocked shelves (canned food, water, batteries, first-aid).
- **Darkroom section:** blackout curtains, red safelight, chemical trays
  (developer, stop bath, fixer), clothesline with binder clips, drying negatives.
- **Scanning station:** flatbed scanner (chunky 2000s Epson), light table, loupe.
- **Satellite dish:** roof-mounted, compact, green link light. Uploads blog entries.
- **Map:** taped above the galley. Blackwood County. Dozens of locations circled.

## The Episode Structure (Repeats Every Episode)

1. **Open in the RV.** Carol preps. Checks the map. Picks the day's target. Heads out.
2. **Explore the location.** Atmosphere, discovery, one emotional beat, one lore beat.
3. **Return to the RV.** The darkroom ritual. Develop film. Scan. Type the blog.
   Voiceover reflects. Upload over satellite. Tag: "Entry N."

## The Season Structure — Four Movements

**Movement 1 — The Personal (Eps 1–3):**
House, School, Gas Station. The human scale. Emotional foundation.

**Movement 2 — The Town's Soul (Eps 4–7):**
Church, Police, Library, Town Hall. The institutions. Lore deepens.

**Movement 3 — The Town's Body (Eps 8–11):**
Firehouse, Diner, Clinic, Post Office. The working town. Gentler, melancholic.

**Movement 4 — The Reveal (Eps 12–13):**
Plaza, Plant. The public heart, then the source. Carol confronts where it all
started and delivers her conclusion.

## The Central Mystery

The contamination cleared by 2010, but the evacuation order was never rescinded.
**Why is the town still sealed?**

The answer is grounded, not supernatural: the town was used as an entropic
exhaust vent (a "grounding wire") for Abscissa Industries' harvesting operations
elsewhere. The "air contamination" was Cold Electricity fallout. The evacuation
was a corporate cover-up. And the order was never rescinded because nobody in
Abscissa cared enough to file the paperwork. The town was sealed and forgotten.

Carol never learns the full truth. She gets close. She finds the word "Abscissa"
on a letterhead. She notices the spotty stasis pattern. But she doesn't know
about the Hollow Spaces or The Architect. The audience knows. Carol doesn't.
That's the dramatic irony.

## The Stasis Field — Rules

The stasis is NOT everywhere. It's spotty. It follows buried infrastructure.

| Preserved (Stasis Active) | Decaying (Stasis Inactive) |
|---------------------------|----------------------------|
| The bomb shelter (underground, sealed) | The outdoors (grass, shed, swing set) |
| The house's electrical grid (wiring, outlets) | Organic materials (couch, carpet, clothes) |
| The bathroom fluorescent light | The attic (boxes, dress, albums) |
| The grandfather clock (electric, plugged in) | The woodshop (sawdust, tools, birdhouses) |
| The fridge (still humming) | Food, paper, photographs (water-damaged) |

**The Pattern Carol Notices (but can't explain):**
"Everything that's underground or wired into the walls... it's fine.
Everything organic, everything loose, everything just sitting out... it's gone.
It's like the town is preserving its own skeleton but letting the flesh rot."

## Runtime Guidance

- Small locations (gas station, diner, post office): 7–9 min
- Mid-size (church, library, clinic, firehouse): 10–12 min
- Large (school, police, town hall, plaza): 12–16 min
- Finale (the plant): 16–20 min
- Total season: ~2 hours 25 minutes
